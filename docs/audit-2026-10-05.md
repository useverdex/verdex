# Verdex audit, 5 October 2026

Internal review of everything shipped in Phase 2 so far (boxes 1 to 6): the Solidity contracts on Robinhood
Chain and Base, and the Base and Solana front-end flows. Written by the engineering agent that built them,
with an independent adversarial pass over the contracts by a second reviewer. This is not a third-party audit
and the site says so; it is the list of what was checked, what was found and what was changed.

## Contract findings

The independent pass read every contract in full plus the front-end callers, queried the live Lend
deployment, and found no reentrancy, callback-authentication, rounding-direction, approval, fee-math, decimals,
cap or clone-initialisation problems (details in "Verified correct" below). It did find the following. Status
says what was done tonight.

| # | Severity | Finding | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C-1 | Critical (as reported) | `executor.key` is committed to the repository, while the README says the key lives outside the public repository. | **Not a leak**: the file is in `.publicignore`, and a fresh clone of the public mirror was checked tonight and does not contain it. It is only in the private repository. Recommendation stands: keep the key only in the Railway environment variable, delete the file, and rotate the executor if the private repository is ever shared. README wording corrected. |
| H-1 | High | Lend's oracle silently fell back to the spot price when `observe` reverted. On a sub-second chain an attacker writing one observation per block for a few thousand blocks can push the oldest observation inside the window, after which borrow and liquidation prices are the manipulable spot of a thin pool. | **Fixed in Lend v3.** No spot fallback: pricing uses the longest window the pool still has if it is at least half the configured one, and reverts (`NoOracle`) below that. `createMarket` refuses pools with fewer than 1,800 observations. The deployed v2 has this flaw; it holds no liquidity, and must be replaced by v3 before any is supplied. |
| M-1 | Medium | Liquidation handed collateral over at the higher of spot and TWAP, so during a real drop the lagging average made liquidations unprofitable for up to 30 minutes. | **Fixed in Lend v3.** The health test keeps the higher value (resists one-block dumps); the hand-over price (`seizePrice`) is the lower of the two, floored at TWAP less the bonus, so a dump cannot seize more than about twice the bonus either. |
| M-2 | Medium | No bad-debt handling: a position whose collateral ran out left phantom debt accruing forever, and at full utilisation the raw subtractions could panic. | **Fixed in Lend v3.** When a liquidation takes the last collateral the remaining debt is written off against the reserves first, then the suppliers (`BadDebt` event). Utilisation is clamped at 100%, and available liquidity is computed with an explicit check. |
| M-3 | Medium | One frozen or paused component made an index irredeemable for everyone (`redeem` reverts if any transfer fails). Tokenized stocks carry issuer freeze controls. | **Fixed in VerdexIndex** (the version the Base factory deploys): `redeemForfeiting(shares, to, skipped)` hands out every other stock and leaves the skipped one's units in the contract. Simulated on Base. The Robinhood Chain indexes run the previous code; migrating them means new indexes from a new factory. |
| M-4 | Medium | Treasury's price floor is a same-block spot and the executor supplies arbitrary router calldata, so a sweep can be sandwiched for up to the slippage band. | Open. Mitigation today: the executor only sweeps small amounts and the band is 8%. Fix planned: a time-weighted floor and a spender allowlist in the next Treasury version. |
| M-5 | Medium | Reserves of the stock-loan (short) markets were skimmed to the Treasury, which can only swap USDG/WETH, so those tokens would be stuck. | **Fixed in Lend v3.** Each market has a `skimTo` sink, the treasury by default, that the owner can point elsewhere for stock-loan markets. Reserves are protocol revenue, never user funds. |
| L-1 | Low | `closeLong` reverted after `addMargin` had repaid the whole loan (repay of zero debt reverts inside the callback); closes also revert when proceeds do not cover the debt. | **Fixed in Leverage v2.** The callback skips the repay when the debt is already zero; `addMargin` on a long caps the repayment at the debt. An underwater close still needs margin first; the page says so. |
| L-2 | Low | A disabled Lend market blocked `addCollateral`, so a borrower could not defend a position while a market was paused. | **Fixed in Lend v3.** Collateral can always be added; only supply and borrow are gated. |
| L-3 | Low | Leverage limits in the README did not match the contract (long 2x fails LTV after fees, UI uses 1.9x; shorts had no hub-side cap). | Documented: longs up to 1.9x, shorts up to 2x in the UI; Lend's LTV is the hard limit in both cases. |
| L-4 | Low | Constructors did not validate addresses (a zero treasury would make every fee-paying trade revert). | **Fixed in VerdexIndexRouterCL** (the Base router) before its first deployment. Lend and Leverage already checked. The deployed Robinhood router has non-zero values. |
| L-5 | Low | `spotIn`/`spotOut` ignore the pool fee and price impact. | By design; the page adds the fee and 1% per leg on top, and the router enforces the per-leg limits. Documented. |
| L-6 | Low | The Leverage page closed positions with `minOut = 0`. | **Fixed.** The page passes today's equity less 3% as the floor. |
| L-7 | Low | Owner parameters take effect instantly (no timelock); a single EOA owns everything. | Open, documented on the pages. A timelock is on the list once the contracts are past their first weeks. |
| L-8 | Low | Treasury hard-codes Uniswap v4's storage layout for the pool slot. | Verified at deploy time against a known price; noted. |

Informational items (uninitialised account implementation, dust, `to == 0` accepted, the 1-wei VERDEX fee
waiver through aggregators, pragma pinning) are recorded in the reviewer's report and left as is.

### What the reviewer verified as correct

Reentrancy locks on every state-changing entry point; swap-callback authentication in both routers and the
leverage account (`msg.sender` must be the pool set immediately around the swap, resolved from the canonical
factory or the Lend market); output-before-callback sequencing in the leverage flows; rounding in the protocol's
favour everywhere (`amountsIn` ceil, `amountsOut` floor, borrow shares up, repay shares down, seized floor);
Lend's accounting invariant across all operations and immunity to share-inflation; access control (no owner
function moves user funds in Index, Lend or Leverage); oracle math (`sqrtP²/2^192` both orderings, `TickMath`
constants, floor division for negative tick deltas); exact approvals that are consumed or reset; fee math and
caps; ERC-20 return-value and balance-delta handling (fee-on-transfer tokens rejected); decimal-agnostic code;
liquidation parameter checks; EIP-1167 clone bytecode and same-transaction `init`.

### Base-specific points from the review

Slipstream keeps the `uniswapV3SwapCallback` signature, so the callback check carries over; Slipstream fees are
dynamic, so per-leg limits are the only protection and the spot views will differ from fills by the current
pool fee; the 8-decimal tokens bound the rounding error at one raw unit per component; the `0xef` native tokens
pass the factory's code check and the balance-delta check in `issue`; USDC on Base is blacklistable, so a
blacklisted router or index address would halt trading there. The reviewer recommends a one-share buy and sell
on Base after every deployment as the smoke test, since nothing on Base can be forked locally.

## Base front end and router variant

Checked:

- The Coinbase stock tokens on Base have contract code `0xef`: Base nodes execute them natively. They behave as
  ERC-20 to callers (transfers, balances and approvals succeed in live simulation) but cannot run on Anvil or any
  other EVM that is not a Base node. Every test of the Base flow therefore ran against live state with
  `eth_simulateV1` on `mainnet.base.org`, which supports it. Nothing about the router depends on the tokens
  being ordinary contracts beyond the ERC-20 interface.
- Slipstream pools are keyed by tick spacing. The router's `fees[]` array carries tick spacings on Base
  (`legTier` in `src/lib/indexChains.ts`); the front end never passes a Uniswap fee there. `slot0` has six fields
  on Slipstream and the CL router's interface matches.
- The pool fee shown on the deploy page is read from the pool's `fee()` (500 = 0.05%), not inferred from the
  tick spacing.
- 8-decimal stocks: units per share are computed with the token's own decimals everywhere (`planTemplate`,
  the deploy page, `readIndexes`), and the live simulation issued exactly 100 shares for 99.998 USDC.
- VERDEX does not exist on Base, so the router is deployed with `verdex = address(0)` and the fee waiver
  branch is skipped (`verdex != address(0)` guard); the UI shows 0.25% for everyone.
- The treasury on Base is an EOA (the fee wallet) until a treasury exists there; the fee lands in USDC and is
  moved to Robinhood Chain by hand to buy VERDEX. This is a trust assumption the docs state.
- Wallet switching: `ensureChainOn` moves the wallet to chain 8453 before any Base transaction and the receipt is
  awaited on a Base RPC, not on Robinhood Chain's.

Open items:

- The Base contracts are not deployed yet. Deploy from `/deploy/index/base` with the dev wallet (needs ETH on
  Base), then put the two addresses in `scripts/index-addresses.mjs` (`BASE_INDEX_*`) and verify both with
  `CHAIN=8453 node scripts/verify-contract.mjs VerdexIndexFactory 0x…` and `…VerdexIndexRouterCL 0x…`.
- Base public RPCs rate-limit; the site uses three in a fallback list. A dedicated RPC key would remove the
  occasional "reload in a minute".

## Solana front end

Design: no Verdex program on Solana. A basket buy is N Jupiter swaps (USDC to each xStock), one v0 transaction
per leg, signed in one wallet prompt, sent together, confirmed by polling. The stocks land in the wallet as
themselves. There is no custody, no index token and nothing of Verdex's on the chain.

Checked:

- Every transaction is built in the browser from Jupiter's `swap-instructions` response, with Jupiter's own
  compute-budget and setup instructions, and compiled with the address lookup tables Jupiter names. Seven buy
  legs and two sell legs simulated successfully on mainnet for a real funded wallet, including the fee transfer.
- The fee (0.25% of the USDC side, only when `VITE_SOLANA_FEE_WALLET` is set) is a `TransferChecked` of USDC
  with an idempotent create of the fee wallet's USDC account in front of it. On a buy it rides before the first
  swap (the wallet already holds the USDC); on a sell it rides after the last swap, paid out of the USDC that
  swap produces, so a wallet with no USDC can still sell.
- Slippage: 1% per leg, enforced by Jupiter's `otherAmountThreshold` inside the swap. Price impact above 1% on
  any leg refuses the whole trade before signing.
- Partial execution is possible by design: legs are separate transactions. The page shows which legs landed
  with links, and the balances refresh so the user sees exactly what they hold. There is no way to make N swaps
  atomic without a program, and that is said on the page.
- Blockhash expiry: all legs are sent immediately after signing, then polled together, so a slow confirmation
  on one leg does not expire the others. If the block height passes `lastValidBlockHeight` the page reports
  which legs did not land; those moved nothing.
- Public RPCs: `getTokenAccountsByOwner` is blocked on publicnode, so balances are read from derived associated
  token accounts in chunks of eight with `getMultipleAccounts`, with RPC fallback. xStocks are Token-2022 (with
  a permanent delegate held by Backed, which is the issuer's right to freeze or claw back under their terms);
  USDC is classic Token. Both programs are handled.
- Wallets: Phantom, Solflare, Backpack through their injected APIs (`signAllTransactions`), any other through
  the Wallet Standard (`solana:signTransaction`). The EVM wallet is never used for Solana.
- Baskets only include stocks whose Jupiter liquidity is above 100,000 USD today: MU, AMD, TSM, INTC and RDDT
  were dropped from the Solana baskets for that reason.

Open items:

- Set `VITE_SOLANA_FEE_WALLET` to a Solana address Verdex controls to turn the fee on; until then the page says
  "no Verdex fee on Solana" and charges none.
- Jupiter's free tier (`lite-api.jup.ag`) is rate-limited per IP; heavy use would need a key and the paid host.
- An index token on Solana (a program that holds the stocks and mints shares) is the eventual goal; it needs a
  Rust program and an audit before it should hold anyone's money, which is why it was not built tonight.

## Summary of changes made during the audit

- `contracts/VerdexLend.sol` (v3): no spot fallback in `_prices`, `NoOracle` error, `MIN_CARDINALITY` check in
  `createMarket`, `seizePrice`, bad-debt write-off in `liquidate`, utilisation clamp, `_available`, `skimTo`
  per market and `setSkimTo`, `addCollateral` allowed on disabled markets. Fork-tested: supply, borrow, interest,
  liquidation, repay, withdraw, skim, caps and access checks all pass.
- `contracts/VerdexLeverage.sol` (v2): callback skips `repay` when the debt is zero; `addMargin` caps the
  repayment at the debt. Fork-tested (see below).
- `contracts/VerdexIndex.sol`: `redeemForfeiting`. Simulated on Base: 10 shares redeemed skipping NVDAc, the
  other six stocks delivered, the NVDAc units left in the index.
- `contracts/VerdexIndexRouterCL.sol`: constructor rejects zero factory, quote token or treasury.
- `src/pages/LeveragePage.tsx`: close transactions carry a floor of equity less 3%.
- Artifacts and metadata repacked for all of the above; the Robinhood Chain `VerdexIndexRouter` and
  `VerdexTreasury` are unchanged and still match their verified deployments.

### What this means for the live deployments

- Lend v2 (`0x5B6d…DD0b`) and Leverage (`0x2763…3Ef5`) on Robinhood Chain carry H-1, M-1, M-2, M-5, L-1 and L-2.
  They hold no liquidity. Redeploy both from `/deploy/lend` and `/deploy/leverage` (the pages now carry the v3
  and v2 bytecode), put the new addresses in `scripts/lend-address.mjs` and `scripts/leverage-address.mjs`,
  verify them, and only then supply liquidity. Until then the Lend and Leverage pages point at v2.
- The Index contracts on Robinhood Chain (factory, router, four indexes) are unchanged and fine; M-3 applies
  to them (a frozen component would block redemption of that index) and is accepted for now at the current caps.
- Base deploys the fixed Index and CL router from the start.
- The Treasury keeps M-4 for now; sweeps are small and executor-driven.
